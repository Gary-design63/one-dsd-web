# Operationalizing equity: canonical program foundation

September 8, 2026. Internal program foundation requested by Gary Banks. This is a program interpretation grounded in the sources below, not rewritten DHS policy.

## Definition

**Operationalizing equity is the ongoing, participatory practice of making equity a concrete part of how work is designed, decided, resourced, carried out and reviewed. It means identifying and changing policies, procedures, practices and power relationships that create avoidable barriers or unequal opportunities, and learning with the people affected whether those changes improve access, participation and outcomes.**

This definition links the program's knowledge, learning, consultation, practical tools and collective work to everyday decisions. Awareness and respectful interaction matter; the practical question is also what changes in how work is done and who can influence that change.

## Source basis

The publicly linked DHS Equity Policy is version 2.0, effective August 4, 2023, retrieved September 8, 2026. It addresses equity in routine actions and decisions, including budgets, workforce practices, technology, procurement and service delivery. Its definitions connect equity with power, access, opportunity and outcomes. It also emphasizes ongoing participation and collaboration with affected communities. The policy assigns responsibilities to equity directors and other agency roles. This program supports people in their work; it does not create those agency responsibilities or make participation in this program compulsory. [Published DHS Equity Policy](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/equity-policy_tcm1053-646921.pdf).

Minnesota's Equity Analysis Tool treats analysis as a practical method for incorporating equity into decisions, programs, processes and budgeting. It recognizes different starting conditions and barriers, and includes participation that ranges from informing through collaboration and empowerment. This supports shaping operating decisions with colleagues while they remain open. [State of Minnesota Equity Analysis Tool](https://mn.gov/oeoa/resources/equity-analysis-toolkit/).

GARE connects a shared vision, common understanding, relationships and organizational capacity with concrete changes to policy and practice. Its approach includes evidence about results and centers people affected by racial inequity. This program draws on that approach while retaining its own voluntary participation model and broader disability, language, access and inclusion context. [GARE approach](https://www.racialequityalliance.org/who-we-are/our-approach).

These sources were read directly. Their methods inform this definition; they do not supply a launch date, a records-retention decision or a mandate to use One DHS/One DSD People, Access and Culture.

## What the definition means in practice

The program should help a person or team work through eight connected questions, using as much support as the situation calls for:

1. What is open to change, and who can influence the decision?
2. Who experiences benefits, burdens or barriers differently, and what structural conditions help explain that?
3. Whose knowledge is missing, and how can those people shape the options?
4. What does evidence establish, and what remains uncertain?
5. Which alternatives address the underlying barrier or distribution of opportunity, resources and influence?
6. What support makes the approach workable?
7. What changes in the actual work, with responsibility agreed rather than assumed?
8. How will people hear what happened to their input, examine effects and adapt?

These are the program's companion questions, not a replacement sequence for the official toolkit.

For example, a team exploring meeting participation might examine who can influence the agenda, which formats enable contribution, and whether contributions affect decisions. A team reviewing a form might examine avoidable documentation burden, language and disability access, and whether people can actually complete the intended task. A workforce discussion might examine access to developmental assignments and the decision practices that shape those opportunities. These are illustrative possibilities, not assigned projects or predicted results.

## Connection to existing program components

| Component | Contribution to operationalizing equity |
| --- | --- |
| ASK | Clarify the actual decision, examine options, explain evidence and connect useful support |
| Learning and podcasts | Develop practical understanding that people can apply voluntarily |
| Community and DHS references | Supply context while preserving the need to learn directly from affected people |
| Equity Toolkit companion and practice paths | Help prepare analysis, alternatives and usable work products |
| Consultation | Support complex work when the service is ready and the person chooses it |
| One DSD Team and Amplify | Enable shared inquiry, relationships, contribution and meaningful report-back |
| Chief of Staff | Prepare and complete authorized support tasks, maintain records and surface unresolved decisions without manufacturing agreement |
| Program evaluation | Distinguish delivered support, its application and observed benefit; use evidence to improve the program itself |

The toolkit companion already addresses decision scope, affected people, alternatives, participation and review. Operationalizing equity is therefore not entirely missing from the program. The improvement is to make its meaning explicit and consistently connect those existing elements.
