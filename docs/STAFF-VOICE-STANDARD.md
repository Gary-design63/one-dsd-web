Requested technical subject matter, code, and formulas in ASK answers are allowed; the ban on internal implementation chatter is not a topic ban.

This is a release requirement for every staff-facing page, form, answer, notice, error, community brief, learning path, and collaboration area.

## Current staff experience, September 7, 2026

The owner's exact tone is **warm, human, professional, and helpful**. Respect intelligent, educated end users. Keep the interface minimal: use only buttons and controls that serve a clear purpose, and remove unnecessary system instructions, technical vocabulary, and robotic wording.

## Builder discussions and on-page instructions

Shop discussions between Gary and the builders stay out of staff-facing copy. Do not turn corrections, implementation decisions, limitations described during development, or internal system and agent instructions into public explanations. Fix the underlying issue; do not narrate the development conversation to staff.

Put instructions and steps in the user guide. Keep on-page guidance only when the person needs it at that moment to complete an action or make an informed decision. Use concise, natural wording.

Preserve existing source material and useful subject-matter content. Do not apply a blanket deletion rule to professional guidance, examples, or source narratives. Retain necessary action-specific privacy information and honest failure notices.

## Editorial reference and audience

Use Minnesota Department of Human Services [Content and writing guidelines](https://mn.gov/dhs/digital-showcase/content-guidelines/) as the editorial reference for content we author or adapt. DHS emphasizes first-read understanding, descriptive sentence-case headings, familiar terms, clear structure, and writing suited to the reader. Its [communication product standards](https://mn.gov/dhs/general-public/policies/standards-for-vendors.jsp) also call for active voice, concise sentences, and explanations of necessary technical terms. Both sources were checked September 6, 2026.

The DHS public-content guideline generally aims at grades 5–6 when the subject allows. The owner has instead specified approximately grades 10–12 for this staff program's substantive content. That is an audience decision, not a DHS requirement or a minimum complexity score. Keep headings, navigation, and short messages as simple as their purpose allows. Preserve professional depth and explain specialized concepts without assuming a particular degree or talking down to readers.

These are internal authoring rules, not content to display in the application. Following this reference does not represent agency approval or connection to DHS systems.

## Community briefs: dignity and participation

Introduce communities through specific, sourced examples of creative work, knowledge, leadership, or participation, alongside practical service guidance. Do not define people by disparities, treat them as objects of pity, romanticize them, or imply that one organization represents everyone. Staff may belong to the communities described. Explain documented barriers without turning population-level findings into assumptions about individuals. Keep openings brief, use expandable detail, and connect reflection to actual work. Tribal sovereignty and required consultation guidance remain visible and distinct from general cultural learning.

## Preserve original material

Keep DHS-authored source documents, slides, policy text, quotations, and required wording intact. Apply this standard to our introductions, summaries, course narration, descriptions, and other program-authored text. Do not silently replace an original with a rewrite or change the meaning of a source. Treat an adaptation as an adaptation in the existing content records. Do not perform a corpus-wide word substitution.

## The voice

The program speaks to capable public servants. Its language is warm, steady, respectful, and specific. It helps people understand what matters, what they can do next, and when another person should be involved. Its reasoning may be sophisticated, but its expression stays in plain public-service English at roughly a 10th- to 12th-grade level.

The program does not imitate a person, manufacture intimacy, flatter the reader, or use sentimental language. Warmth comes from clarity, respect, useful context, and choices that preserve the reader's agency.

Invite engagement without demanding ideological agreement or judging a reader's motives. State the equity, inclusion, and accessibility purpose honestly; respectful language must not conceal that purpose or dilute evidence, responsibilities, or program integrity. Support growth from minimization toward acceptance and adaptation through explanation, reflection, and practice. Do not label, diagnose, or rank staff by assumed beliefs or intercultural stage, and do not shame people for asking questions. Preserve voluntary participation where it is voluntary and accurately describe requirements where they exist.

## Required qualities

- Lead with the subject and what matters to the reader. Do not turn every introduction into instructions for using the application.
- Explain the reason for a safeguard without scolding.
- Use complete, natural sentences and familiar words.
- Name limits plainly and without defensiveness.
- Distinguish reviewed program material, current public sources, and official authority in language a staff member can act on.
- Preserve exact program, division, administration, committee, legal, and policy names.
- Use invitations and choices when more than one sound path exists.
- Keep errors calm: say what happened, what remains available, and what to try next.
- Retain earlier wording when it already expresses the purpose clearly. Do not add copy merely to fill a layout.
- Keep a homepage introduction brief. Put detailed directions in the staff guide; keep essential privacy information and action-specific help where readers need them.

## Language that stays off the staff side

- Model, vendor, or product-provider names.
- Agent, digital-twin, prompt, retrieval, routing, orchestration, and other internal reasoning terms.
- Server, browser tab, storage, runtime, deployment, environment-variable, endpoint, payload, schema, fixture, debugging, and similar implementation language.
- Internal contracts, provenance notes, migration notes, build status, test status, and release commentary.
- Conversations with the owner, design decisions, writing instructions, progress reports, or descriptions of what a developer or assistant is doing.
- Unexplained abbreviations, icon-only instructions, Markdown syntax, and machine-like status messages.
- Claims that the program is an official agency system or that a draft is official policy.

Owner-only operating controls may name the minimum technical detail the consultant needs to make an informed operating decision. Those details must not be serialized into or displayed on staff-facing pages.

## Review gate

A change does not pass because isolated banned words were removed. The complete interaction must read naturally in context. Automated checks catch known prohibited terms and public-payload leaks; a human editorial pass checks tone, continuity, respect, and practical usefulness across routes.

Read the text aloud. Remove vague phrases such as "Bring the work in front of you" and unnecessary procedural descriptions such as "practice with a useful work product." Say what the content is about. Check that technical accuracy, source meaning, required privacy information, and accessible control labels survive the edit. A readability score is a diagnostic, not proof that writing is clear or respectful.
