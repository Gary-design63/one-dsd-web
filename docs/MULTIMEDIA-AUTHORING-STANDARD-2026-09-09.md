# Multimedia authoring direction

Current owner direction, September 9, 2026. Internal authoring and verification guidance.

## Purpose and placement

Select a medium for the understanding, engagement or practical action it supports in an exact resource. Record the course, lesson, scenario, tool or page and its specific placement. Consider the content immediately before and after it so the experience has a coherent flow. Do not add pictures to fill space or repeat a generic group portrait across unrelated topics.

Use varied, relevant subjects: people where interaction matters; locations, neighborhoods, buildings, objects, documents, symbols, charts or data tables where those illuminate the subject. A Rondo discussion calls for relevant, sourced neighborhood context rather than an unrelated workplace photograph. Historical photographs and public figures need accurate attribution, context, rights and dates. Generated reconstructions must never be described as authentic documentary photographs or imply endorsement.

## Photographic standard

The approved landing photograph, public/images/minnesota-communities-group-v2.png, and existing approved e-learning photographs are quality references. Preserve the hero unchanged. New representational images must be photorealistic, with natural proportions, believable skin and fabric, plausible light and setting, and coherent hands, faces and eye-lines. Avoid cartoon characters, plastic-looking faces and caricatures. Match the quality, not the same group composition in every asset.

Native charts, diagrams, maps and data tables remain appropriate functional visuals. Their labels and accessible equivalents must carry their meaning. Fictional figures and scenarios must be identified as fictional, without inventing DHS performance or causal findings.

## Disability and community representation

Disability can be visible or nonvisible. A wheelchair is not the default symbol for every disability-related topic. Wheelchair users belong in relevant scenes, alongside varied ordinary participation and context-specific barriers and supports. Do not infer diagnosis from appearance, label real people's disability without an appropriate basis, or require disclosure to make an image meaningful.

No person, pose, garment, symbol or single organization represents an entire cultural community. Community knowledge provides context for asking and learning, not a diagnosis of an individual. Use specific, sourced context and preserve dignity and agency.

## Tools and delivery

Blender is excluded from this initiative. Choose appropriate non-Blender tools for photographs, original visuals, recordings, video, interactive components and existing-source media. Tool availability does not prove a finished asset, a license or a working application connection. Keep tool/provider discussion in authoring records rather than staff-facing lessons.

Preserve original source content, course blocks, saved learner answers, publication scope and existing actions. Add companions without shifting block-indexed learner records. Retain clear source links and applicable policy wording. Accessible text, labels, captions, transcripts or data equivalents should provide the same useful information. Do not use autoplay, motion or decorative complexity that interferes with the task.

For each exact resource, record: purpose; placement; source or generation provenance; rights status when relevant; accessibility alternative; authored/integrated/verified state; checks; and unresolved gaps. Record an explicit reuse decision when existing media or text already serves the purpose. A queued assignment is not a completed enhancement.

Current authorization covers local creation, integration and verification with team agents. It does not add purchases, commits, pushes or live deployment to this pass. Later direct owner instructions govern any scope change.
