# Advanced diversity course research and authoring evidence

Verified September 22, 2026. Internal evidence; not staff-facing copy.

Ten original CoursePacks, forty lessons, 120 observable objectives. Each lesson has a fictional case, exactly one recommended scenario response, reasoned feedback, a knowledge check, private reflection, an applied next step, and concept cards. All have four teaching paragraphs; teaching word counts exclude repeated structure, objectives, feedback, and prompts. Total teaching words: 13815. Job aids have four sections and exceed 150 words.

The educational writing and fictional cases are original applications. Sources provide named theories, bounded empirical findings, and limitations; they are not represented as approval of this program. No human reviewer is invented. Governance reviewers are empty. Levels remain open, voluntary, and distinct from individual IDI assessment. No Native American-specific material was authored.

The reused photograph was visually inspected: five colleagues around a white conference table in a glass-walled room. Alt text describes observable content, not inferred identities.

## Aversive Racism and Discrimination Under Ambiguity

Lesson teaching words: 348, 341, 352, 349. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Dovidio and Gaertner (2000), Aversive racism and selection decisions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11273391/): Abstract and citation record. Experimental selection research supports examining ambiguous decisions; simulated decisions do not diagnose individual staff.
- [Dovidio, Kawakami, and Gaertner (2002), Implicit and explicit prejudice and interracial interaction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11811635/): Abstract and citation record. Primary interaction study. Measures and impressions are context-dependent; workplace exercises here are program-authored applications.
- [Dovidio and Gaertner, full article](https://cpi.stanford.edu/_media/pdf/Reference%20Media/Dovidio_Gaertner_2000_Discrimination.pdf): University-hosted full primary article for optional examination of the study design and limits.

## Whiteness as an Institutional Norm: A Critical Examination

Lesson teaching words: 355, 360, 355, 356. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Harris (1993), Whiteness as Property](https://harvardlawreview.org/print/no-volume/whiteness-as-property/): Foundational legal analysis of racialized value and institutional protection; not a statement of current workplace legal obligations.
- [Ray (2019), A Theory of Racialized Organizations](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122418822335): Publisher record; full-text access may depend on the publisher or your library. Organizational theory supports examining resources, agency, credentials, and formal-practice gaps. Local applications require local evidence.
- [Harris, Whiteness as Property — teaching excerpt](https://kristelia.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Harris-Whiteness-as-Property.pdf): Selected passages from the original legal article, hosted as a teaching reading. This is an edited excerpt, not the complete article or current legal guidance.

## Intersectional Discrimination: Advanced Case Analysis

Lesson teaching words: 359, 361, 344, 358. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Crenshaw (1989), Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/uclf/vol1989/iss1/8/): Foundational primary legal scholarship on the limits of separate race and sex categories. Cases here are original learning examples, not legal determinations.
- [Crenshaw (1991), Mapping the Margins](https://evallab.unm.edu/restricted-materials/crenshaw-1991---intersectionality.pdf): University-hosted original article on structural and political intersectionality. Its specific context should not be flattened into a universal identity checklist.

## Epistemic Injustice: Whose Knowledge Counts?

Lesson teaching words: 352, 345, 340, 358. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Fricker (2007), Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing](https://academic.oup.com/book/32817): Primary philosophical framework. The distinction between testimonial and hermeneutical injustice informs the original workplace examples.
- [Dotson (2011), Tracking Epistemic Violence, Tracking Practices of Silencing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1527-2001.2011.01177.x): Publisher record; full-text access may depend on the publisher or your library. Primary philosophical analysis of silencing. Silence in a particular workplace cannot be diagnosed from this framework alone.
- [Fricker, Epistemic Injustice — author-posted introduction](https://www.mirandafricker.com/uploads/1/3/6/2/136236203/introduction.pdf): Open introduction posted by the author. It introduces the book’s concepts; the publisher link is a book record, not an assurance of free access to the complete book.

## Racial Battle Fatigue and Cumulative Identity-Related Stress

Lesson teaching words: 352, 345, 348, 341. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Smith, Allen, and Danley (2007), Assume the Position… You Fit the Description](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0002764207307742): Publisher record; full-text access may depend on the publisher or your library. Primary qualitative research on racial battle fatigue among African American male college students. Its sample and setting do not establish prevalence or diagnosis among DHS staff.
- [Nunez-Smith and colleagues (2007), Impact of race on the professional lives of physicians of African descent](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17200221/): Abstract and citation record. Primary qualitative workplace research describing racialized professional experiences and fatigue. It does not justify individual clinical conclusions.
- [Pascoe and Smart Richman (2009), Perceived discrimination and health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19586161/): Abstract and citation record. Research synthesis provides context for discrimination-health associations, not a clinical screening or causal diagnosis of an individual.
- [Pascoe and Smart Richman, open full review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2747726/): Open research synthesis for optional reading about methods and limits. Associations do not diagnose an individual or establish the cause of a person’s symptoms.

## Restorative Responses to Identity-Based Harm

Lesson teaching words: 336, 328, 351, 330. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [UNODC (2020), Handbook on Restorative Justice Programmes, second edition](https://www.unodc.org/documents/justice-and-prison-reform/20-01146_Handbook_on_Restorative_Justice_Programmes.pdf): Authoritative guidance on voluntary participation, safeguards, and program design. Its justice-system context is distinct from these program-authored workplace exercises.
- [Strang and colleagues (2013), Restorative justice conferencing systematic review](https://www.campbellcollaboration.org/review/restorative-justice-conferencing-recidivism-victim-satisfaction/): Research review on face-to-face criminal-justice conferencing. Outcomes in those studies are not evidence that a workplace identity-harm meeting will be effective or appropriate.

## Social Networks, Homophily, and Opportunity Hoarding

Lesson teaching words: 342, 340, 340, 332. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [McPherson, Smith-Lovin, and Cook (2001), Birds of a Feather: Homophily in Social Networks](https://sites.santafe.edu/~aaronc/courses/5352/readings/McPherson_Smith-Lovin_Cook_01_BirdsOfAFeature_HomophilyInSocialNetworks.pdf): University-hosted full research review distinguishing network composition and homophily. ## Designing and Testing Bias-Reduction Interventions

Lesson teaching words: 347, 329, 338, 341. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Devine and colleagues (2012), Long-term reduction in implicit race bias](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23524616/): Abstract and citation record. Primary habit-breaking intervention study; findings are specific to its design and measures, not a guarantee of workplace behavioral change.
- [Lai and colleagues (2016), Reducing implicit racial preferences: II. Intervention effectiveness across time](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27454041/): Abstract and citation record. Primary comparative intervention research emphasizes distinguishing immediate changes from persistence. It does not support employee scoring.
- [Devine and colleagues (2012), open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3603687/): Open-access primary article for examining methods and limits directly; optional further reading.

## Intergroup Contact: Conditions, Power, and Unintended Effects

Lesson teaching words: 339, 347, 346, 344. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Pettigrew and Tropp (2006), A meta-analytic test of intergroup contact theory](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737372/): Abstract and citation record. Research synthesis supports attention to contact conditions; it does not guarantee the result of a particular workshop.
- [Paluck, Green, and Green (2019), The contact hypothesis re-evaluated](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/behavioural-public-policy/article/contact-hypothesis-reevaluated/142C913E7FA9E121277B29E994124EC5): Review of randomized studies with delayed outcomes, including important limits on generalization.
- [Tropp and Pettigrew (2005), Relationships between intergroup contact and prejudice among minority and majority status groups](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16313659/): Abstract and citation record. Research on differing relationships across group status; used to prompt examination of unequal benefits and burdens.
- [Pettigrew and Tropp (2006), full article](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/pettigrew_tropp_2006_-_intergroup_contact_theory.pdf): University-hosted full research article for optional methods and evidence reading.

## Moral Exclusion, Dehumanization, and Rehumanization

Lesson teaching words: 336, 338, 344, 348. Estimated lesson time: 4 × 14 minutes; further reading optional.

- [Opotow (1990), Moral Exclusion and Injustice: An Introduction](https://spssi.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-4560.1990.tb00268.x): Publisher record; full-text access may depend on the publisher or your library. Foundational theoretical account of the scope of moral concern. Workplace cases are original applications, not claims about particular employees.
- [Haslam (2006), Dehumanization: An Integrative Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859440/): Abstract and citation record. Theoretical research review distinguishing forms of dehumanization; not a diagnostic classification for staff.
- [Haslam and Loughnan (2014), Dehumanization and infrahumanization](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23808915/): Abstract and citation record. Research review covering mechanisms, evidence, and limitations. No single wording choice establishes a person’s motives.
- [Haslam (2024), Dehumanization and mental health](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11083880/): Open author editorial on everyday dehumanization and agency. Its clinical discussion is contextual reading, not a clinical protocol or evidence that these workplace exercises improve health.

## Explicit evidence limits

- Aversive racism: experimental patterns do not diagnose a colleague or prove motives in one decision.
- Whiteness and intersectionality: scholarly frameworks require local evidence; they are not current legal advice or a basis for assigning group character.
- Epistemic injustice: silence has multiple explanations and cannot establish consent or a diagnosis of silencing on its own.
- Racial battle fatigue: qualitative findings from students and physicians do not establish staff prevalence or individual clinical diagnoses. No forced disclosure or distress scoring.
- Restorative responses: criminal-justice evidence is not a guarantee for workplace identity harm; participation is informed and voluntary, with formal complaint/support routes preserved.
- Networks: professional-platform experiments do not guarantee internal advancement effects; no friendship surveillance.
- Intervention testing: changes in implicit measures are not automatically durable behavior change. IAT remains optional private reflection, never personnel assessment.
- Contact: proximity and positive ratings do not prove lasting change; power and minority-status burden require attention.
- Dehumanization: original workplace applications are not clinical protocols; labels and language alone do not prove motives.

## Independent review corrections

All forty knowledge checks were rewritten after independent review found that their initial answer options repeated the worked scenarios. Each now asks a distinct conceptual or transfer question, has three answer options with one supported answer, and includes reasoned correct/incorrect feedback. Correct-answer position varies. Programmatic verification confirmed forty unique questions, 120 unique answer texts, and zero exact answer-option overlap with the corresponding scenario. Original scenarios and substantive teaching were preserved.

The second lesson in Designing and Testing Bias-Reduction Interventions now uses the narrower third objective: “Identify when a proposed evaluation needs appropriate research or ethics guidance.” This matches the lesson’s actual teaching and does not imply that it supplies a complete regulatory test distinguishing service improvement from research. The correction script is preserved as advanced-authoring/refine-checks.py.
