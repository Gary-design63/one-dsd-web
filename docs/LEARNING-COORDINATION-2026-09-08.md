# Learning coordination: canonical program direction

September 8, 2026. Internal program and implementation guidance for One DHS and One DSD People, Access and Culture.

## Owner intent and authority

The owner has directed a coherent, foundations-first intercultural learning experience that helps people use DEIA education in everyday work. The program should connect existing resources and future user-directed modules through a meaningful progression, make those connections available through ASK, and support the Chief of Staff's coordination. The question is what people can do with the learning, how they can examine its use, and what they might learn next.

The owner's current direction applies alongside the [DIGITAL MING directive](OWNER-DIRECTIVE-2026-09-07.md), [working agreement](WORKING-AGREEMENT.md), [staff voice standard](STAFF-VOICE-STANDARD.md), [operationalizing-equity foundation](OPERATIONALIZING-EQUITY.md), and [program operating charter](PROGRAM-OPERATING-CHARTER.md). This document establishes the present learning coordination direction. It does not impose a schedule, participation requirement, assessment label, training-credit entitlement, or new approval hierarchy over owner-curated content.

The owner has also ended the request to obtain DHS internal Office of Employee Culture Emerging Leaders materials. The completed public-source analysis and DSD development remain useful. The historical research boundary stays documented; it is no longer an outstanding requirement for this work.

## Logic model

The program's organizing sequence is a learner's need or question → relevant learning and activity → a useful work product or planned change → application and reflection → evidence that informs the next action. It supports repeated exploration, not a one-way completion ladder.

| Element | What it includes | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs and context | A question, goal, unfamiliar situation, access barrier, decision, or developmental goal the person chooses to bring | A stated need supplies context. It does not establish a diagnosis, orientation, deficit, or predetermined learning level. |
| Inputs | Approved learning, original sources, official tools, community context, practical experience, professional support, and colleagues' perspectives | The resource must actually be available in the relevant scope. An archive entry alone does not establish availability. |
| Activities | Substantive explanation, realistic examples, observation versus interpretation, perspective-taking, practice, feedback, and reflection | Each newly authored focus or lesson needs meaningful observable objectives and activities that address them. Participation remains voluntary. |
| Work products | A respectful question, revised conversation plan, participation approach, draft analysis, practical note, or reasoned next step | A created or saved artifact establishes an output. It does not establish that the person used it, that others agreed, or that an improvement occurred. |
| Application and reflection | Try a proportionate change where the person can act; consider feedback and what happened; retain, revise, or seek another perspective | A participant's account can provide application evidence with its source and limits. A choice, click, or completion checkbox cannot supply this evidence by itself. |
| Intended outcomes | Better-informed communication and decisions, greater ability to adapt practice, more accessible participation, and learning that carries into comparable work | These are intended contributions. Describe an observed effect only when evidence exists, and do not infer a causal organizational result or IDI movement from course completion. |
| Ongoing learning | Return to a relevant focus, select further learning, use ASK, or join an optional professional or peer conversation | Return is an invitation. It creates no compulsory sequence, activity deadline, staff ranking, or commitment from another person. |

This logic model guides internal design and evaluation. Staff pages show the useful purpose, examples, practical choices, and ways to continue in natural language. They do not reproduce this planning discussion or label the internal machinery.

## Five shared learning focuses

The current editable journey, `learn.intercultural`, is registered at `/learn/intercultural`. Its five focuses are shared across One DHS and One DSD through the existing publication and inheritance rules. They are available from approved static defaults in the local preview; this does not claim live publication of this pass.

| Focus | Staff-facing title | Practical contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundations | Begin with curiosity | Distinguish observation from interpretation, notice a familiar expectation, and frame a respectful question. |
| Perspectives | Make room for different experiences | Examine unequal effects, identify a missing perspective, and reconsider an initial interpretation. |
| Communication | Adapt how you communicate | Ask about preferences, adapt an approach to a stated need, and check whether it helped. |
| Decisions | Carry learning into a decision | Identify an open decision, compare alternatives, and connect evidence, participation, and responsibility. |
| Reflection | Learn from what happens next | Distinguish activity from effect, describe evidence without overstating it, and choose a next adjustment. |

Each focus currently supplies three observable objectives, an everyday example, practice, a reflection question, possible evidence, selected existing learning and tools, and an optional next step. The complete published course inventory remains available. The six existing learning-stage links connect into the relevant focus or orientation rather than imposing prerequisites.

The companion practice notebook supports a draft, optional saving on the device, a later reflection, copying or downloading, and removal of the saved copy. Its intended product is a personal learning note, not a staff assessment, institutional decision, or official training record. Saving, return behavior, error handling, and downloads require their own verification receipts; code presence alone is insufficient.

## Open participation and IDI context

People can follow the foundations first, choose any useful focus, explore another resource directly, or return to an earlier idea. There is no minimum tenure, required nomination, prior assessment, or course-completion gate for this learning.

Someone who has an IDI profile and individual development plan may use goals from their debrief to choose learning. Someone without an IDI assessment has the same access. The program must not infer an orientation from identity, questions, clicks, activity, or completion. It must not diagnose or rank a person, turn an original course title into their label, promise progression to another orientation, or equate a completed module with assessed intercultural development.

The official IDI description identifies Qualified Administrators, individual and group profiles, and personalized development plans. That supports connecting optional program learning to a person's existing development goals without reproducing or replacing licensed assessment or qualified feedback. [IDI products and services](https://www.idiinventory.com/products-and-services), checked September 8, 2026.

Original course names that refer to Minimization, Acceptance, or other concepts remain original course names. Our surrounding descriptions explain their practical subject and welcome any learner; the program does not assign those names to visitors.

## Training-credit rule

The canonical shared notice is maintained in `lib/program/learning-credit.ts` and must be reproduced consistently when relevant:

> Participation and course completion in this program do not count toward DHS-required training credits unless management, a director, or DHS leadership expressly approves an exception.

ASK, the Chief of Staff, a completion marker, a manager's encouragement to learn, and a generated practice note cannot create or imply an exception. An actual exception requires the relevant authorization; the program must not claim one without evidence. Optional learning and this credit rule do not waive existing DHS duties, required training, or applicable Equity Analysis Toolkit obligations.

The State of Minnesota Equity Analysis Tool describes a method for incorporating equity into decisions, policies, programs, processes, and budgeting, with participation from affected people. The program's companion helps people apply learning and prepare their work; the original tool and applicable policy remain the sources for official requirements. [State of Minnesota Equity Analysis Tool](https://mn.gov/oeoa/resources/equity-analysis-toolkit/), checked September 8, 2026. The [published DHS Equity Policy](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/equity-policy_tcm1053-646921.pdf) is the separate policy source already documented in the operationalizing-equity foundation.

## Program connections and reasoning

The journey links learning with program orientation, Minnesota Communities, the Toolkit companion, guided practice, Learning for your work, Amplify Equity, One DSD Team and its Learning Lab, and DSD leadership development. Their functions differ: context can improve a question; a tool can help draft a decision; a peer conversation can inform reflection; professional support can help when another perspective is useful. A link does not imply a booked meeting, an accepted volunteer task, a mentoring match, or staff consent to share a note.

ASK should answer the actual question, reason from available context, and offer a small useful selection with reasons and a practical next step when appropriate. It remains capable of broader questions and external research when separately configured and applicable. This learning pathway is not a closed topic boundary. An exact request for an existing named resource must continue to open that resource instead of being diverted into a generic journey.

Current integration supplies ASK with the published journey text, the five supported section destinations, and the shared participation context. `getLearningJourneyContext` requires the journey's current published document before exposing its guidance and section links. Course overviews and lessons already enter the scoped program index. Recommended resource links must match the available scoped destinations, and unknown or withdrawn links must not be reintroduced through fallback suggestions. A failed reasoning request must be described as unavailable or limited; a relevant navigation fallback is not a completed tailored analysis.

The Chief of Staff's operations-summary context obtains the DSD journey through the same current-publication index and receives the shared credit rule. It can use those connections when identifying useful support or coordination. This integration does not establish that a learner completed work, a colleague accepted an assignment, a benefit occurred, or a particular orientation was reached. Source presence is not a receipt of real agent work.

## Staff-facing language and internal design

The staff voice remains warm, human, professional, and helpful. Describe what a person can explore and use, give enough subject-matter depth to support learning, and avoid commentary about our design choices, agents, source ingestion, tests, or publication machinery. Instructions belong in the guide unless the person needs a short explanation at the current action. Useful privacy information, the training-credit qualification, and honest failure feedback remain available where relevant.

Use a calling-in approach: invite questions, consider evidence, and examine decisions and practices without shaming people or inferring motives from identity. Community context helps prepare questions and understand conditions; it is not a prediction about an individual. Application should be proportionate to the learner's purpose and available influence, with no invented obligation to complete an extensive work project.

## Preserved content and remaining curriculum work

The source census found 86 recovered courses containing 780 lessons, all registered as course surfaces. Original recovered packs, the course manifest, and the candidate archive were not rewritten by this coordination work; the audit retains hashes and accounts for every course candidate ID. Existing course tiles, lessons, interactions, and source history remain part of the program.

That preservation does not establish that every lesson is already fully developed to the current standard. The full structural census found 254 lessons sharing one applied-next-step sentence and only five distinct evidence strings across all 780 lessons. The displayed objective helper synthesizes three bullets and does not itself prove that each objective has distinct teaching, practice, and feedback. The selected foundational courses also rely on scenarios, transfer choices, and notes rather than their own artifact blocks; connecting them to practical tools is therefore useful.

The new journey supplies coordination and a specific application/reflection experience. It does not constitute a full rewrite or content-quality certification of all 780 lessons. Deeper, user-directed modules remain an explicit curriculum task: develop substantive foundations and specific skills; align three to five observable objectives with explanation, examples, practice and feedback; provide a useful application; and make subsequent reflection meaningful. Preserve original materials while adding traceable authored content and improvements. The [gap analysis](INTERCULTURAL-LEARNING-GAP-ANALYSIS-2026-09-08.md) and its linked inventory retain the detailed findings.
