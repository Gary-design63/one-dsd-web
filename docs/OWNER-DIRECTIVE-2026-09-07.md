**Audience:** Internal program and implementation guidance; this document is not staff-facing copy

## Purpose and exact operating concept

**DIGITAL MING** is the owner's exact term. Preserve its capitalization and wording; do not replace it with a different name or reinterpret it as a new product brand.

DIGITAL MING is a virtual simulation of the owner's professional knowledge, judgment, and approach: a digital partner that extends the reach of his equity, inclusion, and human-services work across approximately 4,000 agency staff. The owner continues the face-to-face relationships, presence, and professional work that require him. The program helps him reach people and carry work forward at a scale he could not cover individually.

The resource tool serves both One DHS People, Access and Culture and One DSD People, Access and Culture. The owner describes One DSD as the pilot and proving ground, selected by him and DHS leadership, and developed in tandem with the agencywide One DHS program. This records the owner's account of that decision. It does not claim independently verified agency endorsement or create authority to act as an official DHS decision maker.

DIGITAL MING is the canonical operating concept. Keep the existing staff-facing program names, logo, page design, and content intact unless the owner requests changes. The concept authorizes a professional digital partner; the application must still accurately distinguish its own responses and actions from the owner personally speaking, meeting with someone, or making an official agency determination.

## Chief of Staff and program autonomy

The Chief of Staff is the owner's DEIA / Equity and Inclusion Operations Consultant partner. It coordinates, delegates, follows work through completion, resolves routine issues, and gives the owner a clear view of what happened. It is not an additional approval authority over content the owner has already approved.

The owner wants maximum autonomy within this program, with agents doing real tasks in every area. The operating target is **90–95% agent-driven work**. An A0-to-A2 MVP ceiling, a proposal-only A5 interpretation, or an off-by-default build setting must not be presented as the owner's desired operating model. Configuration defaults and missing adapters are implementation matters to reconcile, verify, and report.

Use the owner's existing authorization to complete routine program work without needless repeated approval. Retain the owner's ability to stop, inspect, or reduce activity. A task should not stop merely because another AI or the Chief of Staff has not reapproved the owner's supplied content.

The 90–95% figure is a target, not a verified achievement. Measure it against an explicit reporting period and a declared set of eligible program tasks. Report completed agent work, human interventions, failures, excluded tasks, and the reason for each exclusion. Do not inflate the result by counting suggestions, repeated retries, or queued work as completed actions. The older 90–95/100 or 95/100 release-quality scores are separate measures.

## Owner-curated content is approved

The owner states that he personally curated and reviewed all content he put into this program. That content is already approved. This supersedes older language treating the same content as approved only for intake, or requiring a new editorial, AI, Chief of Staff, or human content-approval round before routine program use.

Record the approval that already exists, with its owner, scope, date, and the affected item or revision. Do not fabricate a new review, pretend an agent performed a human review, or mark an untested technical check as passed. Preserve source files, original wording, revision history, attribution, and publication/withdrawal records.

Approval does not make a broken asset, inaccessible interaction, incorrect link, or missing resource functional. Repair those defects under the owner's instruction and provide evidence. If an actual unresolved issue prevents completion, say precisely what failed and what remains. Do not convert that reporting duty into a generic request for the owner to approve the same content again.

This direction covers the content the owner supplied and curated, including the existing collection. It does not invent approval for private staff submissions, confidential case details, credentials, or material from another person that the owner has not supplied or approved. Nor does it authorize silently rewriting original institutional source material or claiming institutional rights and authority that have not been established.

## ASK breadth and observable work

ASK is intended to help an end user with any question; it is not limited to retrieval from a reviewed library or to a fixed list of program topics. It must combine reasoning, the program's resources and destinations, and external research when the question calls for them. Requested technical explanations, code, or formulas are legitimate answer content. Internal implementation chatter must still stay out of ordinary staff guidance.

All eligible program resources must be accessible through ASK or correctly routed from it. Resource status and scope matter: withdrawn, unavailable, or out-of-scope items must not be falsely presented as available. Answers must not claim research was performed or a source was consulted when it was not.

The owner has explicitly authorized completing the Perplexity API setup and generating the necessary keys without exposing secrets. **Sonar is not an option.** The owner excluded it. Do not reinstate Sonar through a preset, fallback, or model substitution. The owner's statement about its future support is the reason he gave; this document does not independently establish a deprecation date.

ASK responses and other agent work must have observable, durable records. The owner must be able to inspect the question or task, the response or actual action, its outcome, supporting resource/source references where applicable, and a receipt identifying the work. A trace containing only a hash or a promise to do work does not satisfy this requirement.

This supersedes an absolute prohibition on server-side ASK records and the claim that consultations are the only persisted staff-authored work. It does not turn those records into a shared conversational memory, an employee scoring system, or a basis for exposing one staff member's question to another. Store the records through the appropriate access-controlled program boundary, describe the recorded interaction accurately, and preserve applicable redaction, deletion, and retention behavior. No new retention duration or indefinite-retention authorization is invented by this decision.

Record failed and degraded operations as such. A displayable response is not proof that its persistence succeeded. A completed task must have a real result and a matching receipt; a failure to save, route, research, or execute must be visible and addressed.

## Staff experience and podcasts

ASK is for all staff and end users. It must be approachable without requiring people to understand the program's internal systems or manage its agents.

Podcast authoring belongs to Gary, the Chief of Staff, and the agents. Staff simply play the podcasts. Do not add staff authoring or management controls merely because the underlying program supports them.

Keep the staff interface minimal and useful. Avoid unnecessary buttons, system instructions, technical vocabulary, and robotic language. Respect intelligent, educated end users and make each control serve a real purpose. The owner's exact tone is **warm, human, professional, and helpful**.

This direction does not remove concise information a person needs to make a decision, such as whether their ASK interaction is recorded or what sharing a request does. Put that information at the relevant action, in natural language, without turning ordinary pages into system instructions.

## Keep builder discussions out of staff copy

Shop discussions between Gary and the builders stay out of staff-facing copy. Corrections, implementation decisions, limitations discussed during development, and internal system or agent instructions must not be rewritten as public explanations. Resolve the issue in the program instead of publishing the development conversation.

The user guide holds instructions and steps. On-page guidance belongs only where a person needs it at that moment to act or make a meaningful decision. Keep those explanations concise, warm, human, professional, and helpful.

Respect existing source material and useful subject-matter content. This instruction is not a blanket deletion of guidance, professional depth, or source meaning. Nor does it remove concise, relevant privacy information or truthful notices when an action fails.

## What done looks like

The owner asked for the entire local application to be evaluated: every workflow, button, route, resource, and relevant agent capability. Smoke testing, verification, and stress testing must produce receipts. Repair broken wiring, missing assets, silent failures, and bottlenecks found in that work. Include a completed task and its receipt in every agent area; registry entries, mocks, or plans alone do not establish that agents do the work.

Report the tested environment and distinguish local evidence from a production result. Production deployment is complete only after a successful deployment and verification of the deployed application. An attempted deployment, preview, successful local build, or unverified hosting response must not be described as deployed.

The owner's later deployment authorization follows the requested repairs and verification. It does not remove their required order, and a later pause or narrower instruction must be respected. This documentation update itself does not perform or claim a deployment.

Communicate promptly when an issue is discovered. Say what is verified, what is incomplete, and what evidence supports the conclusion. Do not hide failures behind a success message or defer material problems until after saying the work is done.

## Program authority and execution controls

The owner's authority governs the program's intended behavior. It does not disable the managed permissions, tool approvals, or execution controls of Codex or the hosting environment. Do not claim those controls have been removed. Work within existing authorization and applicable execution controls, using an available permitted route; identify a concrete blocked action and the actual reason if one remains.

Within the application, authentication, scoped access, immutable source history, actual technical validation, the owner's stop control, and honest evidence remain useful operating controls. They must not become a substitute approval hierarchy over already approved owner content.

The Chief of Staff's program authority does not by itself authorize contacting other people, sending outside communications, making formal HR/case/complaint determinations, speaking for a sovereign Tribal Nation, or exercising official DHS powers. Those actions require their own actual authority and task scope. These distinctions must not be used to refuse ordinary learning, research, explanations, or routine authorized program tasks.
