ONE DHS PEOPLE, ACCESS AND CULTURE PROGRAM
Workforce relevance and One DSD Team | 6 September 2026
Owner working document. Not staff-facing copy or agency policy.

Purpose
Connect organizational context, position responsibilities, current tasks, learning, and practical equity work. Preserve this connection through changes in leadership, funding, staffing, and organizational arrangement. The One DSD Team provides a practice-based contribution to this continuing program, not a parallel engagement group.

Research and coverage
DHS public directory: https://mn.gov/dhs/about-us/contact-us/agencywide-organization/
MMB overview: https://mn.gov/mmb/employee-relations/career-paths-and-families/classification-specifications/
MMB class indexes reviewed: spec-h.jsp and spec-s.jsp under that directory.
The public directory identifies organizational areas and leadership titles. It does not establish every unit, position allocation, duty, or reporting relationship. The initial code baseline records agency areas and the DSD/ADSA relationship without claiming that agency-area membership means a direct reporting line.
Ten classification titles/codes are recorded as index references. Their individual specification documents and position-specific duties have not yet been analyzed. They are not assignments of those classes to DHS units.
The work categories used for recommendations are program-authored categories, not official MMB job families. County, Tribal, provider, and state employees must not be conflated merely because their tasks intersect.
The entire agency workforce map is NOT complete. A leadership directory plus selected classification references cannot establish a complete workforce inventory.

One DSD Team operating program
Approximately 20 members with varied expertise, according to the owner. No named roster is invented.
The consultant provides substantive program direction. Members contribute experience, examine processes, review resources, and help test practical changes. Managers and other responsible officials retain their decision authority.
General meetings: shared priorities, one substantive question, relevant learning, and updates on accepted work.
Open hours: voluntary help with a question or work in progress; not a replacement for the general meeting.
Between meetings: bounded contributions, resource review, and returning what was learned.
Do not publish dates until confirmed. Do not assign volunteer work solely from job title.

Work cycle
Identify a real question -> understand the process and affected people -> examine evidence and resources -> agree a proposed improvement and authorized decision owner -> try when authorized -> review results -> retain, revise, or stop -> continue.
For each item retain: question, scope, task, relevant resources, evidence limits, contribution owner, decision owner, next check-in, and outcome. Do not store case records, personal complaints, or participant profiles.
Possible first work items: a confusing form or notice; a meeting participation barrier; a resource that does not fit an actual staff task. These are proposals, not an assigned workplan.

Amplify connection
Amplify remains staff-directed. Co-leads carry only agreed questions, with minimal identifying detail, to an appropriate recipient. The One DSD Team may consider a question but is not automatically its implementation owner. Co-leads return the response. No bulk copying of engagement conversations, transcripts, or personal stories.

Organizational model required for completion
Separate organizational area, official classification, working position, responsibilities, tasks, competencies, resources, and permissions.
Stable identifiers outlast display names. Preserve dated relationships and replacements rather than deleting history when an area reorganizes.
Vacancy is a position state, not removal of its responsibilities. A retirement is not permission to infer a successor.
Responsibilities may cross units; resources may serve many tasks without duplication.
Allow unassigned and locally defined roles without inventing official classifications.
Record what was publicly verified, locally confirmed, proposed, or superseded on the consultant side.

Scoped governance required for completion
Administration and division stewards require authenticated, explicit grants. A selected role, title, or organizational preference never grants editing, approval, or publishing rights.
Local stewards propose and maintain their area; shared changes require the shared program's approval rules. No one changes another area's assignments merely by changing a parent selector.
The owner's ADSA exception remains a specific permission-design question; no administration-wide exception is inferred from DSD responsibility.
Support review, effective dates, history, rollback, and retirement of mappings. Existing two-scope publishing remains unchanged until tested replacements exist.

Intelligent behavior required for completion
Use only voluntarily supplied context. Ask about the task when a role is insufficient. Offer tailored examples, 3–5 observable objectives for a lesson, relevant published tools, and appropriate further research. Keep unrestricted exploration available.
Do not treat a classification description as a person's actual authority. Do not score beliefs, infer identities, or treat missing role data as a deficit.
External research must be distinguishable from confirmed organizational knowledge. Changes discovered externally become reviewable proposals, not automatic organizational facts.
Automated work needs an actual execution record, failure handling, scoped tools, and owner disable control. No background automation is claimed by this increment.

Acceptance work still required

Portability
Retain data definitions, tests, research notes, and acceptance criteria in the existing repository so another coding platform can continue. Do not clone the application to preserve a plan. No official DHS access, live deployment, or database migration is included in this increment.
