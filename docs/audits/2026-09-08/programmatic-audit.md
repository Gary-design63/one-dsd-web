# One DHS / One DSD People, Access and Culture: program audit

September 8, 2026 • Internal owner report • Findings and recommendations, not a new operating directive

## Judgment

**Yes. This is a genuine program by design, supported by substantial working delivery infrastructure. It is more than a website, a course catalog, or an ASK interface. The evidence reviewed does not yet establish the full consistency, reach, or effectiveness of its operation with staff.**

The program has an enduring purpose, intended participants, explicit outcomes, connected interventions, stewardship, a volunteer contribution model, recurring operations, a pilot-to-scale approach, and a method for learning from work. Those are substantive program characteristics. The application supports their delivery; Gary's professional relationships, consultation, facilitated conversations and collective work are also part of the program.

Its principal opportunity is to connect the existing delivery system more consistently to **applied work, observed results, and subsequent improvement**. Building additional content alone would not resolve that gap. Neither would a new name, another committee, compulsory learning, or a high software-test score.

Confidence is strongest in the existence of the design and implemented mechanisms. Evidence of real-world operation is less complete in the material inspected. No numeric program-maturity or effectiveness score is assigned: the reviewed sources do not support a defensible quantitative rating.

## Scope and method

The owner requested a program audit, not another technical smoke test. The lead auditor coordinated three independent supporting agents covering (1) program design and canonical requirements, (2) delivery and participant journeys, and (3) outcomes, management, autonomy and sustainability. Findings were reconciled against current owner direction, original operating documents, code, and dated verification receipts. The lead independently checked material findings about current authority, consultation activation, the Microsoft bridge, and the Team operating cycle.

The assessment covers the agencywide One DHS program and the DSD pilot/proving ground; learning, practice, ASK, community context, consultation, One DSD Team, Amplify, podcasts, organizational knowledge, stewardship, agent execution, evaluation, and continuity. Historical documents were used to recover requirements, with later owner decisions taking precedence.

This was a comprehensive program-level assessment across those areas, not a claim that every historical duplicate, every code line, or every private record was read. The design reviewer read the full separate DSD Operating Model and Superior Operating Program PRD, as well as the embedded product PRD and relevant original contracts. The delivery reviewer computationally examined the entire recovered course collection and inspected delivery implementations and receipts. The outcomes reviewer examined operating controls, agent execution, evaluation and lifecycle evidence. Coverage details and sources appear below.

No private staff records or Teams conversations were inspected, and no colleagues were contacted. A question was sent to Gary about whether the pilot has begun and what activities/results already exist; no answer was available at this report's cutoff. Consequently, **not established in the reviewed records does not mean not happening in practice**. Gary's account of pilot selection with leadership and approximately 20 Team members is retained as owner-reported context.

CDC's program-description framework supports examining need, resources, activities, outcomes, context and development stage. GAO distinguishes ongoing performance measurement from evaluation of how and why a program works. These are analytical lenses, not new DHS requirements or approval processes. [CDC program description](https://www.cdc.gov/evaluation/php/evaluation-framework-action-guide/step-2-describe-the-program.html); [GAO performance measurement and evaluation](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-11-646sp).

## Strengths to preserve

### Applied practice is already a central mechanism

Eleven practice paths connect an actual question to relevant context, resources, a useful work artifact, self-check and optional consultation. They describe intended independent capability and distinguish substantive work from superficial completion. Six learning stages extend from orientation through application, systems practice and continuity. Seven work domains identify work outcomes, rather than simply grouping topics.

This is a strong foundation for moving from knowing about equity and inclusion to doing work differently. The program should preserve open exploration: these pathways are supports, not compulsory levels or access gates.

### The content and DSD depth are substantial

The recovered collection contains **86 courses and 780 lessons**. All 780 include learning, scenario and transfer sections; 164 lessons contain artifact blocks, with 175 knowledge checks and 69 sorting blocks across the collection. Forty-four original audio assets were recovered. Two owner-supplied approved podcasts are connected to relevant learning. DSD has twelve program/function profiles and thirteen scenarios; community briefs add practical context and questions without assuming individuals represent a group.

A September 8 production receipt reports **1,082 course/lesson/media checks with zero failures**. Another receipt reports 87 course-interaction tests passing. Eleven practice paths have local browser workflow receipts. These are strong delivery-availability findings. They do not prove learning transfer or organizational benefit.

### Human and collective delivery have an existing design

The separate DSD Operating Model includes a Program Steward, One DSD Team, monthly meetings, Open Hours, a workroom, quarterly review and annual refresh. The Team addendum describes roughly 20 contributors according to Gary, and a concrete work cycle with contribution owner, decision owner, next check-in and outcome. Amplify includes a structured 45-minute conversation design and follow-through routes.

Connection, reflection and belonging can be legitimate purposes of an activity. Every conversation need not be converted into an improvement assignment. Where an activity does aim to change a practice, however, follow-through should be visible.

### Agent work and organizational memory have real foundations

Chief of Staff code performs resource checks, request triage, packet refreshes, summaries and recorded steps. Recent verification includes three real-provider local answers with durable records and a real-provider cycle using a synthetic consultation. Prior production receipts verify organizational answers and their stored response records.

The newly published Understanding DHS reference has thirty source-linked topics, linked from Learning and Resources, Library, About and relevant ASK responses. Its dated source knowledge can improve coordination. Review-due flags exist; they are not proof that an automatic refresh service is already operating.

### Owner intent supports effective operation

The current directive defines DIGITAL MING precisely, confirms owner-curated content is approved, sets a 90–95% agent-driven target, requires actual results and receipts, and reserves Gary's final judgment. It calls for warm, human, professional and helpful staff language. These requirements are compatible with a strong program: they clarify purpose while allowing routine execution.

## Findings and recommendations

### 1. Reconcile the existing operating model into current authority

**Finding:** Important program requirements are distributed across the active repository and older desktop documents. The good operating model exists, but a future builder can miss it. Older approval rules, proposed structures and owner labels can be mistaken for current instructions.

**Recommendation:** Produce one concise current operating charter and a one-page model linking existing activities to outcomes. Reference preserved originals; identify current decisions and explicitly superseded material. Reconcile, rather than rewrite the entire history into another sprawling PRD. Keep the detailed internal model available to the Chief of Staff without placing builder discussions on staff pages.

**Completion evidence:** Each major program function has an intended beneficiary, outcome, delivery route, responsible role, cadence and feedback route. No contradiction remains about curated-content approval, autonomy or who makes material decisions. Historical partnership proposals remain visibly proposals.

### 2. Close the loop from a useful artifact to a change in work

**Finding:** Practice self-checks largely assess field completeness, minimum detail, owners and dates. Course completion is a participant-selected checkbox. Consultation records include status, scheduling and notes, but no consistent structured account of what was implemented and what happened afterward. Marking a request completed does not prove benefit.

**Recommendation:** Add a minimal program-level change record to selected real work: problem, intended improvement, artifact/action, responsible decision owner, what was adopted, evidence/date, next review, and retain/revise/stop decision. The Chief of Staff can prepare and maintain these records for authorized work. Private learning notes remain private; staff can voluntarily contribute a redacted example or aggregate observation.

**Completion evidence:** A small portfolio of actual DSD work can be followed from initial need through implementation and follow-up. Include unsuccessful attempts and adaptations, not only favorable examples.

### 3. Make Chief of Staff coordination carry work through completion

**Finding:** The system does useful checks and bounded actions, but parts of the improvement cycle end at proposals. Ordinary content or capacity proposals do not all have implementation actions attached. Some registry descriptions retain historical review language. Configured A5 does not establish the owner's desired level of autonomous delivery.

**Recommendation:** For already-authorized routine work, connect finding → assigned agent task → action → verification → result → closure or explicit exception. Use owner involvement for real decisions or missing authority, not repetitive approval of already-curated content. Expand executable work in proportion to actual program needs, including selected collective-work follow-up where authorized.

**Completion evidence:** Reports distinguish actions completed, proposals awaiting a genuine decision, retries, failed tasks and empty checks. No proposal is counted as a delivered improvement. Gary can inspect, redirect or stop work.

### 4. Resolve the difference between available guidance and activated services

**Finding:** Consultation workflow code exists, but the committed activation register remains `blocked_pending_evidence`, with policy/retention and several operating-evidence fields pending. The availability code actually reads that register, so this is consequential configuration, not merely an old note. The native Team workspace supports discussions, actions and feedback behind owner access; fallback data is sample/planned. The Microsoft bridge is explicitly off and cannot read or write Teams. Existing personal Teams channels are separate from automated application integration.

**Recommendation:** Maintain a short internal service-readiness map: available self-service, working owner tools, real-participant service ready, and remaining concrete dependencies. Reconcile inherited approval language with current owner direction and finish genuine technical/operating dependencies under existing authorization. Do not replace unknown evidence with an approved flag. Connect actual face-to-face or Teams delivery where useful; no particular external connector is required to make this a program.

**Completion evidence:** Every advertised service can either deliver its stated result or clearly route to the real available service. Submitted work has a responsible recipient and verified follow-through. This audit does not establish that human consultation or meetings are inactive.

### 5. Measure benefit separately from activity and software quality

**Finding:** The program already names meaningful outcomes. Current receipts mainly establish routes, storage, provider responses and synthetic task behavior. The privacy-safe completion aggregate is still cataloged as a later capability; no handler was found. A populated pilot baseline, follow-up series or verified institutional results was not established.

**Recommendation:** Use a modest evidence plan tied to the existing north-star outcomes. Combine delivery counts with artifact quality, voluntary application evidence, accessibility/usefulness feedback and observable changes in work. Start with realistic contribution claims; broad workforce or service disparities have many causes. Do not equate fewer consultations with independence without checking whether staff could accomplish the task successfully.

**Completion evidence:** Each selected outcome has a defined question, collection method, reporting period, evidence owner and interpretation limits. A review actually changes the program when evidence calls for it.

### 6. Demonstrate the 90–95% autonomy target honestly

**Finding:** The target and its measurement requirements are already explicit in the owner directive. Production configuration has been verified as A5 at a dated check. Earlier ten-agent receipts used deterministic logic; one cycle had no requests to triage or refresh. A later real-provider synthetic cycle is stronger functional evidence, but neither establishes sustained real workload coverage.

**Recommendation:** Define eligible program tasks before each reporting period. Record task purpose, responsible agents, completion receipt, failure/retry and owner intervention. Report both the proportion of eligible tasks completed without required human intervention and actual human effort. Keep complexity and exclusions visible, so trivial automated activity does not conceal the difficult work left to Gary.

**Completion evidence:** A reproducible numerator and denominator, an explicit period, actual completed work, quality/rework information and explanations for exclusions. Tool-call volume and successful empty cycles do not count as completed program tasks.

### 7. Resource continuity and scale deliberately

**Finding:** Stewardship, volunteers and recovery procedures are documented. A local restoration verified 42 tables with matching counts and digests. That receipt explicitly does not verify hosted production recovery. The $200 monthly external-research budget exists, but total service costs, maintenance capacity, backup coverage and actual support allocation were not established in this audit.

**Recommendation:** Keep one lightweight capacity record covering Gary's available time, backup arrangements, agent-owned recurring work, service demand/overflow, total cost envelope, source maintenance and demonstrated production recovery. Use the DSD pilot to identify what transfers agencywide and what needs local adaptation. Scale against service quality, demand, cost and maintainability, not a calendar promise or login count alone.

**Completion evidence:** The program can explain how it continues when Gary is unavailable, a provider fails, costs grow or source material changes. New units receive relevant support without implying unconfirmed authority or staffing commitments.

## A practical operating model for the next cycle

The following is a recommended synthesis of existing requirements, not a newly imposed operating mandate:

**Staff need → relevant support → useful work → application → follow-up evidence → program adjustment.**

Gary and the Chief of Staff identify a small portfolio from real work. Existing suggestions include a confusing form, a participation barrier in a meeting, and a resource that does not fit a task; those are examples, not an assigned workplan. ASK, courses, community context and practice paths support the chosen work. Agents prepare materials and perform authorized tasks. The responsible person applies the change. Follow-up records what happened and the program adapts.

Use the existing monthly meeting and Open Hours to help work progress, quarterly review to examine patterns and revise priorities, and annual refresh for continuity and scope. Avoid a parallel meeting structure. A short rolling offering calendar can connect podcasts, relevant learning and facilitated conversations to actual needs without producing content merely to fill a calendar.

| Outcome question | Proportionate evidence | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did people obtain useful support? | Aggregate service use, successful routing, optional usefulness feedback and unmet requests | Learning or equitable institutional impact |
| Did capability improve? | Voluntary before/after work examples, aligned task rubric and later comparable-task demonstration | Individual merit, beliefs or employee performance |
| Did a work product or process improve? | Documented adopted change, accessibility/task-success observation and follow-up | That the program alone caused the result |
| Did people experience meaningful access and participation? | Voluntary feedback, access-barrier fixes, redacted examples and appropriate aggregate patterns | Representation of everyone who did not respond |
| Did DIGITAL MING reduce burden? | Eligible-task completion, quality/rework, owner interventions and human effort | Value from automation percentage alone |
| Did the program learn? | Recurring need → improvement task → verified change → subsequent result | Improvement from merely generating recommendations |
| Can the program continue and expand? | Capacity, costs, source currency, failure recovery and evidence from the DSD cycle | Agencywide effectiveness from a small pilot |

Protect small groups from identification in aggregate reporting. Do not collect individual learning histories simply because an evaluation dashboard could use them. Useful program evidence can come from selected voluntary work examples and process observations.

## Recommended order

1. **Reconcile and orient:** current charter, existing operating rhythm, actual pilot status and service-readiness map. Preserve Gary's account and avoid re-interviewing him about decisions already settled.
2. **Demonstrate a complete work cycle:** selected real work, executable agent follow-through, optional application evidence and a review decision.
3. **Connect measurement:** a small owner-facing evidence view covering delivery, use, benefit, autonomy and unresolved service issues. Staff pages remain focused on useful work.
4. **Strengthen continuity and scale:** confirm capacity and recovery, maintain source knowledge, refine curriculum-to-practice connections and expand based on pilot learning.

These are recommendations for the next implementation scope. This audit did not change program behavior, permissions, active instructions or public copy. The separately authorized Understanding DHS release was completed and checked during this audit.

## Final assessment

The evidence supports calling One DHS / One DSD People, Access and Culture a **substantively designed program with extensive implemented digital delivery**. The next programmatic advance is to make its existing operating model consistently executable and to show what changes through its use. Demonstrated pilot results, sustained work cycles and accountable agent completion will establish the strength of its operation more convincingly than additional feature volume.

## September 8 owner clarification after this audit

Gary clarified that launch is anticipated in early 2027, subject to change, and that operating decisions require input from colleagues, including the approximately 39 equity professionals he described. Participation is voluntary across DHS, Aging and Disability Services and DSD. No launch deadline, participant deadline, meeting schedule or 90-day staff-record retention period was approved.

This changes the interpretation of readiness findings: capabilities needed for eventual delivery can be prepared now, while operating arrangements remain open to collaborative design. Absence of launch-stage participation or outcome evidence must not be treated as a current program failure. Existing meeting and review patterns are planning options rather than imposed commitments. The program can use agreed review conditions as well as dates when people choose them.
