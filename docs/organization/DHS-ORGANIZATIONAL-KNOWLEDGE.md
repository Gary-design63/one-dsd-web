# DHS organizational knowledge for the Chief of Staff

Research date: September 8, 2026. Reference version: 2026-09-08.1.

## Purpose and authorized scope

Gary authorized a thorough study of Minnesota DHS, with deeper Disability Services coverage, and integration into the program's Chief of Staff knowledge. Completion requires usable evidence in answers and coordination, with inspectable receipts. This extends DIGITAL MING's organizational understanding. It does not revise the application's owner authority, staff design, content approval, research spending cap, or provider exclusions.

## What the study establishes

The reference contains 30 organizational topics and 34 official public sources. It covers purpose, organizational functions, agency boundaries, strategic direction, county and Tribal relationships, Medicaid, aging, disability supports, behavioral health, housing, employee culture, access, operations, reporting, grants and legislative work.

DHS is a network of program, operational and partner responsibilities. A useful answer must distinguish the service, the decision and the organization responsible for it. DSD program expertise, provider licensure, Medicaid enrollment, individual support planning, and separate agencies are not interchangeable.

## What is connected

- ASK selects relevant organizational topics alongside its existing published program resources. The answer request receives the source facts, checked dates, relevant functions and separately labeled application suggestions. Only supplied, used citations may be returned.
- Organizational knowledge survives process restarts as a versioned repository data file. It is not a chat transcript or shared memory of staff questions.
- The Chief of Staff's existing work cycle builds organizational briefs for eligible open requests, supplies them to its operations reasoning, and records the reference version, source links, possible coordination needs and refresh status in the existing cycle receipt.
- The cycle still performs its actual existing specialist work: accessibility checks, content review, request-packet refresh and queue triage. An organizational suggestion is not falsely counted as an external contact or an additional delegated task.
- Every eligible open request receives an organizational brief. Repeated topics are deduplicated for the generated operations summary; the receipt retains per-request accounting.

## Source conflicts and limits

- **internal relationships:** Public sources do not establish current DSD internal teams, informal working relationships, delegated decision authority, or the pilot's internal sponsor and operational contacts. Add Gary's role-based relationship map and authorized internal materials when available. No private staff directory was inferred.
- **legacy dsd placement:** The older DSD About us page says Continuing Care Administration; the agency directory and CBSM introduction say Aging and Disability Services. Use the corroborated Aging and Disability Services placement; retain the legacy source as historical evidence, not current structure.
- **leadership currency:** The agencywide directory and older executive biographies differ on Aging and Disability Services leadership. The directory contains vacancies and interim appointments. Use role-based routing. Recheck the live directory for person-specific questions; no permanent officeholder list is embedded.
- **strategy scope:** The agencywide public page presents the 2023–2027 plan. A 2026 capital-budget document labels a different set of treatment-oriented goals 2026–2028. Keep the agencywide plan and capital-budget document distinct. Do not silently replace one with the other.
- **restricted operations:** PartnerLink and internal operational systems may require authorized access. Public source research does not establish access to their contents. Use public manuals and directory routes now; add authorized internal material with its actual access scope.

## Maintaining the knowledge

The dated facts and program-authored application suggestions remain distinct. Fast-changing directory and implementation sources have a seven-day check interval; other operational sources use 30 days, and selected stable historical or background sources use 90 days. These are maintenance intervals for this program, not DHS policy. Source checks becoming due appear in the Chief of Staff receipt. A source coming due does not erase its knowledge or stop coordination. Its facts remain available as a dated reference, with a refresh flag; changing details require current verification.

The source-check script retrieves all registered URLs and records response status, final URL, byte count and SHA-256 identity. It does not change a checked date or approve facts just because a URL returned successfully. A content refresh requires reading the changed source, reconciling affected claims and recording the new checked date and reference version. This work has not installed a separate scheduled external crawler.

Refresh workflow: retrieve sources; identify changes; verify affected facts and links; preserve any superseded claims in source history; update the reference; run scenario and provider checks; publish through the existing release process. Relevant new public research can still be used by ASK under the existing research configuration.
