# Thirty new diversity courses

## Owner request and scope

Gary requested thirty new courses, ten each at Foundation, Intermediate, and Advanced, using the full existing course format. The written proposal in the task is the title and topic authority. The spoken paraphrase included different topics; it is not substituted for the written thirty. The owner explicitly requested multiple agents.

The Foundation, Intermediate, and Advanced authoring agents own separate folders. The coordinating agent owns integration, source-register updates, tests, presentation, release preparation, and verification. Existing courses, community briefs, source archives, and staff answers are preserved.

## Purpose and quality standard

Fill dedicated curriculum gaps concerning privilege, microaggressions, cultural communication and values, implicit bias, social identity, intersectionality, and deeper interpersonal and institutional patterns. Whole-person learning and practical work application both matter.

Every course requires four substantive lessons, three to five explicit observable objectives per lesson, explanation before application, original fictional cases, scenario choices with reasoned feedback, knowledge checks, reflection, an applied next step, a usable job aid, and source references. Source reading is not reproduced wholesale. Source limitations remain visible. A minimum word count is a screening check, not proof of instructional quality.

The three course levels describe material depth. They do not assign an IDI orientation, certify a person, restrict access, or confer mandatory DHS training credit. IAT participation is optional with an alternative exercise; personal results are not collected. Restorative practices never compel harmed people to participate or replace formal processes. The program does not diagnose racial stress, determine complaints, or create agency policy.
