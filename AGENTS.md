# September 14 owner directive: IDI as the program's theory of change

This supersedes the prior instruction not to infer an IDI orientation from program activity. The Intercultural Development Inventory continuum — Denial, Polarization, Minimization, Acceptance, Adaptation, Integration — is the program's explicit theory of change, not background context. DHS staff as an organization sit near Minimization; the program's job is to move people toward Acceptance, Adaptation, and Integration. Build this into the program's identity directly: mission/about copy, leadership content, and course content should name and use these stages, and dedicated content should exist for every stage, not only Minimization and Acceptance.

Two things stay true alongside this:

1. **Never turn an individual's inferred or assessed IDI position into personnel data.** No result, inferred orientation, or movement along the continuum may become an employee record, an access gate, a score, or a hidden label attached to any specific person. Licensed IDI assessments remain arm's-length and professionally administered; this program does not replace, score, or shortcut that. This protects staff, and it is not in tension with using the continuum as the program's organizing framework — one is about content and structure, the other is about individual staff privacy.
2. The whole-person purpose stands alongside the transactional/service-delivery purpose, not beneath it. Content that helps a staff member examine their own culture and movement along this continuum (including and especially dominant-culture staff examining their own default assumptions) is exactly as legitimate a use of this program as content that builds service-delivery competence for a specific client community. Do not treat introspective, personal-growth content as secondary or optional relative to operational content.

# September 14 owner directive: Native American content defers to DHS

All content, imagery, and program decisions involving Native American individuals, communities, or Tribal Nations — including but not limited to the "Native American Minnesotans" and "11 Tribal Nations" community briefs — defer explicitly to the Office of Indian Affairs and to the Department of Human Services offices that handle tribal matters, rather than being authored or finished unilaterally within this program. Say this plainly to staff wherever the gap is visible: this is a standing deference to those offices, not a "brief under construction" or a production-timeline question, and not something this program will complete on its own. Do not independently complete, expand, or publish new Native American-specific content (written or visual) without that coordination. Flag gaps (such as the unfinished Native American Minnesotans brief) to Gary rather than closing them by generating replacement content.
