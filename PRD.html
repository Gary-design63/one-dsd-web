1. Purpose

One DHS People, Access and Culture connects professional learning with everyday decisions, relationships, services, and working conditions. One DSD is the division-specific pilot and reference implementation. Staff should be able to begin with a question, find relevant context, work through a useful example, prepare a practical next step, and return to what happened.

Operationalizing equity means making equity a concrete part of how work is designed, decided, resourced, carried out, and reviewed. The program connects awareness with participation, decision authority, practical change, and evidence. It does not equate attendance, resource views, or completed activities with equitable outcomes.

The owner's operating concept is DIGITAL MING: a professional digital partner extending the reach of the owner's knowledge and practice while preserving his face-to-face role. This is an internal operating concept. Staff-facing names and branding remain One DHS and One DSD. Automated output must not be presented as Gary personally speaking or exercising official agency authority.

Early 2027 remains a tentative launch horizon. Participation and operating arrangements are shaped with colleagues; a proposed cadence is a planning pattern, not a confirmed schedule or an assignment of staff capacity.

3. Program principles

  • Open, voluntary learning. Staff can open a resource or practice activity without completing another course first. Participation remains voluntary except for independently applicable agency obligations. Program completion does not count toward DHS-required training credits unless management, a director, or DHS leadership expressly approves an exception.
  • Respect professional experience. Use warm, clear language with substantive context. Invite questions and examine evidence without shaming people or inferring motives from identity.
  • Preserve approved sources. Owner-curated material is already approved. Keep original institutional text, sources, attribution, and publication history. New program-authored companions sit alongside original material.
  • Keep context distinct from assumptions. Community knowledge prepares staff to ask better questions. It does not establish an individual's language, preferences, cultural identity, disability, diagnosis, or beliefs.
  • Make participation consequential. Explain what is open to influence, who can decide, what changed, and how a response returns. A contributor does not automatically become responsible for implementation.
  • Make status truthful. Authored, integrated, retained, browser-verified, published, and deployed are separate states. Configuration, a draft, or a successful request does not establish a completed outcome.

4. Staff journeys and functional requirements

4.1 Begin and navigate

Home, Start, About, Orientation, and the One DSD entry point provide useful routes into the program. The approved homepage photograph remains unchanged. Navigation should connect the question to learning, practice, community context, or support without creating another directory that duplicates every destination.

The learning and resources collection uses /learn as its primary entry. Existing library and resource URLs must keep working through the application's compatibility routes. Resources can belong to more than one theme while remaining a single maintained resource. Recommendations must resolve against the scoped published collection.

4.2 Learn and apply

Courses retain their original titles, substantive lesson blocks, related resources, and source material. Course tiles use relevant photographs and open the complete resource. Staff guides, learning stages, practice paths, and courses remain distinct.

The intercultural journey offers five useful focuses: curiosity, perspectives, communication, decisions, and reflection. Each focus connects context, an everyday example, meaningful practice, reflection, evidence, and relevant resources. It must not label an employee's IDI orientation or infer developmental movement from program activity.

The Equity Analysis Toolkit companion helps staff examine a decision through its original practice choices and separate drafting area. Community engagement learning connects activity, influence, results, and report-back. Verified policy obligations remain distinct from optional learning.

4.3 One DSD resources

All 12 restored program profiles and 13 restored practice scenarios retain their original source fields. Program profiles connect divisional functions with concrete equity questions. Scenarios retain the situation, what to notice, questions before acting, practical moves, and appropriate people to involve.

The DSD entry map must show only profiles already visible in the scoped inventory. It connects each program with a specific work question, such as access to assessment, the person's stated goals, documentation burdens, or the meaning of complete records.

Leadership learning spans the employee life cycle and includes development and continuity tools. Examples must remain separate from the learner's own reflection and exported plan. The program does not promise advancement or replace selection, HR, accommodation, or labor-relations authority.

4.4 Team, Amplify, and organizational context

The One DSD Team and Amplify provide voluntary routes for conversation, mentoring, working-condition questions, shared ideas, and practical contributions. Examples explain how a question reaches a decision owner and how a response returns. Meeting arrangements and outside community spaces must not be presented as integrated services solely because a link exists.

Understanding DHS retains its source-linked organizational and service context. Relationship tables clarify distinctions among functions and partners without implying a single chain of command. Employee resource group information distinguishes historical references from current directories. Optional contributor recordings require an actual chosen contribution and appropriate attribution; the program must not fabricate an endorsement or ask someone to represent a whole community.

4.5 ASK and support

ASK helps staff ask broad questions, find program resources, consider practical applications, and use external research when an enabled, working research capability supports the request. It must distinguish actual consulted sources from general reasoning and must not claim research that was not performed.

Exact named-resource queries must retain the correct published destination through the complete response. The September 9 repair addresses the case where DSD destinations were present in retrieval but absent from final next actions. Regression coverage checks all 25 restored named DSD destinations.

Related media previews are grounded in already-visible sources. Three exact learning/practice previews and two exact podcast-anchor previews connect the answer with usable examples. Both actual podcast transcripts now enrich their existing scoped published destinations, with a 64,000-character bound per recording and source fingerprints. This is transcript-aware discovery for those two recordings, not transcript search across every program resource.

Support helps the person identify a responsible function or prepare an appropriate request. Consultation and durable submission features depend on their actual configured delivery, storage, access, and failure handling. The interface must describe what is saved or shared at the action where that matters.

5. Multimedia requirements and delivered forms

Choose a medium for the exact resource and learning task. Media should help the learner see a distinction, follow a process, examine evidence, or act. A decorative image added to a category is not an implementation of a resource-specific recommendation.

The September 9 saved map contains 175 recommendation records: 58 selected course/lesson recommendations, 95 DSD recommendations, and 22 broader program recommendations. These are different granularities and are not a complete count of lessons or chapters. Coverage reports must identify the exact resource and delivered medium.

The resource receipts reconcile these rows as follows: 44 selected course additions and 14 reuses; 64 DSD integrations, 30 reuses, and one item awaiting an actual chosen ERG contribution; and 19 broader program integrations with three reuses. These are recommendation/placement counts, not counts of unique assets or a claim that all 780 lessons were newly authored. Production verification is recorded separately.

Delivered forms include native process diagrams and tables; annotated fictional notices and work examples; data views with full tables and interpretation feedback; an accessible fictional form demonstration; a branching service-contact comparison; photorealistic fictional meeting stills; an authentic, attributed Minnesota place photograph; and synthetic narrated dialogue with complete transcripts.

Representational imagery must be photorealistic. Preserve the approved hero. Do not invent documentary historical photographs or imply public-figure endorsement. Disability may be visible or nonvisible; wheelchair imagery must be relevant rather than a universal symbol. Blender is excluded.

Native HTML scenes and comparisons can be the appropriate delivered adaptation of a proposed recording. Reports must say which medium was delivered instead of labeling a written example as a video. Retention is valid when original text, choices, a source link, or existing media already serves the purpose; explain that decision for the exact resource.

Accessibility requirements include meaningful alternative text, semantic headings and tables, keyboard controls, visible focus, clear error feedback, complete transcripts, no autoplay, and usable narrow layouts. Synthetic audio must be identified accurately and must not imitate an actual staff member or simulate a cultural accent or disability. An ASR transcript of an existing recording is a reviewable draft until checked against the audio.

Both original long-form podcasts now have actual recording-derived draft transcripts, passage search, timestamp seeking, and chapter navigation: nine chapters for the equity policy/toolkit recording and 11 for anti-racism in public service. Browser review confirmed actual passage search and playback seeking. The original recordings remain available to verify exact wording; transcript and topic review does not certify every spoken word.

6. Persistence, privacy, and operating controls

Preserve existing notes, answers, completion records, and exports. Lesson companions must not insert or reorder original indexed blocks. Working examples must never populate personal fields or create a hidden submission.

Durable ASK records and task receipts are required under the appropriate access boundary. These records are not shared conversational memory or employee scores. Retention and deletion must follow the actual configured program policy; no new universal 90-day or indefinite-retention rule is created by this document.

The owner retains stop controls and authority over program direction. A 90–95% agent-driven operating model is a target requiring measured task receipts, human interventions, failures, and exclusions. It is not established by a registry setting. External sending, formal decisions, purchases, and new integrations require their actual authorization and capability.

7. Separate second-brain deliverable

The requested second brain applies contextual knowledge, connections, capabilities, and on-demand review to program knowledge and decisions. A local server and isolated API tests exist, with note editing, citations, revisions, connections, and audits described in its own README.

The combined workspace is not yet usable. Automatic approval review rejected installation of 28 source/report files and three staged interface files into the second-brain folder. That restriction remains unresolved. Existing APIs or dry-run import counts must not be presented as a complete program-wide knowledge workspace. There is no claim of connected ASK, email, Teams, or recurring autonomous review.
